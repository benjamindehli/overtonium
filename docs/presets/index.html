Overtonium / Presets

Presets

Twenty-eight ship with the plugin. All but a handful were dialled in on the panel and converted straight from the saved file, so what ships is what was played rather than something written afterwards to approximate it.

What ships

Twenty-eight, and none of them are variations on one another. Just Saw and Equal Saw are the pair worth playing against each other first: identical patches except for TUNE, and they sound nothing alike.

Each one plays the same thing, a spread C major arriving a note at a time and let go together, so they can be listened down rather than each showing itself off in its own way. The clips are rendered by the plugin rather than recorded, so they cannot drift from what it does, and the levels are where the presets put them rather than matched to each other. Nothing downloads until you press play.

2-bit Fuzz Organ
An organ at 8 kHz and 2 bits, where the aliasing and the quantisation noise are the distortion. The modulation wheel or aftertouch blends the root note towards a fifth
Big Saw
A sawtooth with a little randomness in it, so it drifts and beats rather than sitting still
Cathedral
Principal chorus: octaves and fifths only, the registration an organ builder would recognise, arriving slowly as the pipes speak
DigiLog
A digital instrument through tired analogue gear. The envelope delay climbs with the partial, so the series arrives from the bottom up and steps open like a filter rather than sweeping, at 16 kHz and 8 bits
Dire Dire EP
A soft, round electric piano of the kind written for underwater music. Dark except for the 13th and 15th partials, left standing clear of their neighbours as the tine
Drawbar Organ
Nine drawbars, with the key-off stage used as a release click. The upper drawbars jump to 55% for two milliseconds as the contacts break
EP Chimes
A round electric piano with bright chimes above it, at 16 kHz and 8 bits so the artefacts belong to the sound rather than sitting on top of it
Equal Saw
Just Saw with every partial pulled back to equal temperament
FM Piano
The electric piano an FM synthesiser makes rather than the one it is imitating, in the manner of a DX7
Glass Armonica
Wet fingers on glass. Each partial arrives later and vibrates faster than the one below it
Init
The neutral starting point: the first three partials and nothing else. It clears the patch and leaves the session alone
Just Saw
The same sound as Equal Saw with every partial at its exact whole-number ratio
Lo-fi
8 kHz and 8 bits, soft, with very short partials arriving at different times. The territory of a Roland D-50, a Korg Wavestation or a granular synthesiser
Metallic Piano
A thin, metallic upright piano
Music Box
Plucked steel teeth: irregular vibrato per partial, with one bent far further than the rest
Nylon EP
A very round electric piano, as though the tines were nylon rather than steel
Odd Harmonics
Every even partial silent, which is the hollow tone of a stopped pipe. Velocity opens the top of the series
Omni-84
The SonicStrings Voice 2 from a Suzuki Omnichord OM-84 System Two
Shimmer
Every partial breathes at its own rate, so the spectrum never repeats
Slow Pad
Staggered envelope delays, so the spectrum unfolds rather than arriving at once, with mirrored pans across the series
Space Flute
A synthesised pan flute a long way off, with the breath on the noise channel and the distance in the echo and the reverb
Stepped
A slow pad that will not hold still. Seven partials trill on squares set to whole intervals, two at a fifth and five at an octave, while the top three take their level from sample and hold
Struck Bell
Higher partials die away first, which is what makes a struck body sound struck. Strike it harder and the upper partials arrive
StyloPoly
Voice 3 of a Dubreq Stylophone S-1 with the vibrato on, polyphonic as the Stylophone is not. Every partial's level wanders on a smooth random contour of its own
Synth Ensemble
Strings and brass in one patch. A third of a second of attack across the whole series is what makes it an ensemble rather than one player
Tape Choir
Vowel-ish: a formant hump in the low partials rather than a straight roll-off, on an old machine with dark, unsteady repeats
Vibraphone
A fast strike where the fundamental rings for seconds and everything above it is gone inside one
Wurli
A Wurlitzer 200A electric piano

What a preset carries

The patch and nothing else. Everything in the Settings menu stays where you put it, which is the rule rather than a list that might fall behind one: the menu is where the instrument is set up and the panel is where the sound is made. So loading a preset never changes your polyphony, your temperament, your reference pitch, your pitch bend range, your MPE or aftertouch routing, or what leaves the outputs. Nor your master fader, your window size or your LINK settings. Those are properties of the session you are working in rather than of any one sound in it, and having a patch drag you back to equal temperament in the middle of a piece would be no help.

Presets you save follow the same rule as the ones that ship, and carry exactly the same set of controls. A preset saved before this was true still loads, and the settings it recorded are ignored rather than applied.

That includes Init. It is still a preset, so it clears the patch and leaves the session where you set it.

Values are stored plain rather than normalised, so a preset survives a parameter's range being widened later, and anything a file does not mention keeps its default rather than being reset.

Saving your own

Saving writes one small file per preset, and the menu reads that folder every time it opens, so a preset added from outside the plugin shows up without a restart. The folder is created on demand.

Platform Where they live
macOS ~/Library/Application Support/Dehli Musikk/Overtonium/Presets
Windows %APPDATA%\Dehli Musikk\Overtonium\Presets
Linux ~/.config/Dehli Musikk/Overtonium/Presets

They are small XML files holding parameter values, so they can be read, edited, kept under version control and passed to someone else by copying the file. The menu sorts by name rather than by whatever order the filesystem hands them back.

Folders become groups. Make a folder in the preset folder and it appears in the menu as a submenu holding whatever is inside it, nested as deeply as you nest the folders. There is nothing to set up and no second place to manage it: the folders are the groups, so moving a preset between them is moving a file. Groups are listed before the presets sitting loose beside them, and the preset currently loaded is ticked wherever it lives.
