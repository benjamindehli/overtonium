Overtonium / Presets

Overtonium presets

Thirty-eight presets ship with the plugin. All but a handful were set up on the panel and converted directly from the saved file. What ships is exactly what was played, not an approximation written afterwards.

What ships

Thirty-eight presets. Only two of them are versions of one patch, and they are the place to start: Just Saw and Equal Saw are identical except for TUNE, and they sound nothing alike.

Each clip plays the same thing: a spread C major chord, played a note at a time and released all at once. This makes it easy to listen through them in order. The clips are rendered by the plugin, not recorded, so they always match what it does. The levels are the ones each preset sets, not matched to each other. Nothing downloads until you press play.

Thirty-three of them use one of the twelve oscillator characters. The character is part of the patch like everything else here, so what you hear is the preset as it ships. Keep two of the twelve in mind while listening. Bulb is only audible when the pitch moves, and Op-amp only affects partials above a kilohertz. Both do more when you play them on a keyboard than in a clip of a held chord.

2-bit Fuzz Organ
An organ at 8 kHz and 2 bits, where the aliasing and the quantisation noise provide the distortion. The modulation wheel or aftertouch blends the root note towards a fifth
60s Organ
A worn combo organ, with a few milliseconds of key click on every note. It goes into a bucket brigade delay driven so hard that the repeats grow instead of fading, then into a spring reverb long enough to still ring when the next chord starts
6581 Pulse
The pulse wave of a MOS 6581, the SID chip in a Commodore 64, at half its width, which makes it a square wave. It is on Op-amp, which hardens the partials above a kilohertz
6581 Sawtooth
The sawtooth wave of the same chip, on the 6581 character. The character is measured from a real chip, and gives every partial the uneven steps of the chip's converter, the slight bow after it, and its hiss
6581 Triangle
The triangle wave of the same chip, also on the 6581 character. A triangle wave has odd harmonics that fall with the square of their number, and the chip's also has a few faint even ones
Big Saw
A sawtooth with a little randomness in it, so it drifts and beats instead of staying still
Cathedral
A principal chorus: octaves and fifths only, a registration an organ builder would recognise. It fades in slowly, the way pipes start to sound
DigiLog
A digital instrument played through worn analogue gear, at 16 kHz and 8 bits. The envelope delay gets longer for higher partials, so the series comes in from the bottom up. It opens in steps like a filter, not in a smooth sweep
Dire Dire EP
A soft, round electric piano of the kind used in underwater music. It is dark except for the 13th and 15th partials, which stand out from their neighbours as the tine
Drawbar Organ
Nine drawbars on Wheel, with the key-off stage used as a release click. The upper drawbars jump to 55% for two milliseconds as the contacts break
Dream Phase
A strike that turns into a pad, without using separate layers. The short partials make the transient and the long ones come in behind them as it opens. Two partials step on square waves underneath, and the echo is nearly all feedback
EP Chimes
A round electric piano with bright chimes above it, at 16 kHz and 8 bits, so the artefacts are part of the sound and not added on top
Equal Saw
Just Saw with every partial moved to equal temperament
FM Piano
The electric piano sound of an FM synthesizer such as the DX7, not the instrument it imitates, on FM
Glass Armonica
Wet fingers on glass. Each partial arrives later and vibrates faster than the one below it
Glide
A pad that comes in from the bottom up. The fundamental starts at once and every partial above it glides in a little later. Played softly it is round and slow, and played hard it is bright and punchy
Glockenspiel
A struck bar. Nothing sustains, so every partial fades from the moment it is hit, each over its own length of time. The tone comes from the fourth and eleventh partials more than from the fundamental
Init
The neutral starting point: the first three partials and nothing else. It clears the patch and does not change the session settings
Just Saw
The same sound as Equal Saw with every partial at its exact whole-number ratio
KarplusStrong
A single fundamental and the noise channel's Karplus-Strong string, struck together, with the resonator's piano strings ringing in sympathy
Lo-fi
8 kHz and 8 bits, soft, with very short partials starting at different times. Close to the sound of a Roland D-50, a Korg Wavestation or a granular synthesizer
Metallic Piano
A thin, metallic upright piano. The resonator rings the strings of the keys you hold, and every string while the sustain pedal is down
Music Box
Plucked steel teeth, with irregular vibrato on each partial and one partial bent much further than the rest
Nylon EP
A very round electric piano on PD, as if the tines were nylon instead of steel
Odd Harmonics
Every even partial is silent, which gives the hollow tone of a stopped pipe. Velocity opens the top of the series
Omni-84
The SonicStrings Voice 2 from a Suzuki Omnichord OM-84 System Two
Shimmer
Every partial rises and falls in level at its own rate, so the spectrum never repeats
Slow Pad
Staggered envelope delays, so the spectrum builds up gradually instead of starting at once, with mirrored pans across the series
Space Cowboy
A synthetic tremolo guitar, plucked from the string in the noise channel, over a pad with square-wave pitch modulation in some of its partials, and a whistle like a theremin that glides between overlapping notes. The resonator rings under all of it
Space Flute
A pan flute in the distance. The breath is on the noise channel, and the echo and the reverb give the distance
Sparkle Pad
Two instruments, split by where a partial sits in the series. The bottom three partials sustain and take up to three quarters of a second to come in. Everything above them starts at once and is gone within half a second
Stepped
A slow pad that keeps moving. Seven partials trill on square waves set to whole intervals, two at a fifth and five at an octave. The top three take their level from sample and hold
Struck Bell
Higher partials fade first, which is what makes something sound struck. Play harder and the upper partials come in
StyloPoly
Voice 3 of a Dubreq Stylophone S-1 with the vibrato on. Unlike the Stylophone it is polyphonic, but the vibrato is shared across the keyboard, as it is on the original with its single oscillator. Every partial's level moves on its own smooth random path
Synth Ensemble
Strings and brass in one patch. Nothing in it starts at the same moment, and how hard you play sets how far apart things start. A hard chord starts at once. A quiet one swells in over a couple of seconds, with the series drifting apart on the way
Tape Choir
Like a vowel: a formant peak in the low partials instead of a straight roll-off, and an old echo machine with dark, unsteady repeats
Vibraphone
A fast strike where the fundamental rings for seconds and everything above it is gone within one second
Wurli
A Wurlitzer 200A electric piano, with one tremolo circuit for the whole keyboard instead of one per tine, so a whole chord pulses together

What a preset carries

The patch and nothing else. Everything in the Settings menu stays as you set it. This is a general rule, not a list that could get out of date: you set up the instrument in the menu and make the sound on the panel. So loading a preset never changes your polyphony, your temperament, your reference pitch, your pitch bend range, your MPE or aftertouch routing, or Adaptive's Retune, harmonic sevenths and No drift. It also leaves your window size and your LINK settings alone. These belong to the session you are working in, not to any one sound in it, and a patch that reset you to equal temperament in the middle of a piece would get in the way. The master fader and the output stage are different. They are on the panel and part of the sound, so a preset loads at the level it was made at, through the clipper it was made with.

Presets you save follow the same rule as the ones that ship, and store exactly the same set of controls. If a preset file contains any of these settings, it still loads, and those settings are ignored.

This includes Init. It is a preset too, so it clears the patch and leaves the session settings as you set them.

Values are stored as plain values, not normalised, so a preset still works if a parameter's range is widened later. Anything a file does not mention keeps its default value.

Switching them over MIDI

A MIDI program change loads a factory preset, so a keyboard, a foot controller or a clip in your host can change the sound without anyone using the menu. The number selects the entry by its position in the Factory list, counting from zero. The list is alphabetical. Hosts and hardware that number program changes from one are one higher, so their 1 is the first entry. A number past the last preset is ignored. It does not wrap round to the start.

The preset is loaded even if it is the one already showing, as a program change does on a hardware instrument with a panel. So a clip that starts with a program change sounds the same every time it plays, and anything you changed by hand is replaced. If you want to keep those edits, save them as your own preset first.

Only the factory presets have numbers. Your own presets live in a folder you can add to and rename at any time, so their positions would not stay fixed long enough for a clip to rely on them. The same applies across versions. A release that adds a factory preset moves every preset that comes after it alphabetically, so a clip made with an older version can select a different sound in a newer one. Sessions are not affected, because a saved session stores the preset's name as well as its number, and the name is what is used.

Program changes work on any channel, MPE included, and load the same things as loading a preset by hand. Your polyphony, temperament and the rest of the Settings menu stay as you set them.

Saving your own

Saving writes one small file per preset. The menu reads that folder every time it opens, so a preset added from outside the plugin shows up without a restart. The folder is created when it is first needed.

The bar shows the loaded preset's name on a display of nine cells. A longer name is shortened in steps, not just cut off. The spaces go first, then the vowels from the right, and only then is the rest trimmed. So Tape Choir keeps every letter as TAPECHOIR and Glass Armonica becomes GLASSRMNC. The menu, the file on disk and your host all get the full name. Saving does not change the name.

Platform Where they live
macOS ~/Library/Application Support/Dehli Musikk/Overtonium/Presets
Windows %APPDATA%\Dehli Musikk\Overtonium\Presets
Linux ~/.config/Dehli Musikk/Overtonium/Presets

They are small XML files of parameter values, so you can read them, edit them, keep them under version control and share them by copying the file. The menu sorts them by name, not in the order the filesystem returns them.

Folders become groups. Make a folder in the preset folder and it appears in the menu as a submenu with everything inside it, nested as deeply as you nest the folders. There is nothing to set up and nothing else to manage. The folders are the groups, so to move a preset between groups you move the file. Groups are listed before the presets that are not in a group, and the loaded preset has a tick wherever it is.
