# Overtonium 1.7.0

A 32-partial additive synthesiser laid out like a 32-channel mixer. Every channel is one sine oscillator locked to a harmonic of the played note, and every channel has its own tuning, envelope and modulation.

## What is new in 1.7.0

**STRIKE aims key velocity at the front of the envelope**, on every one of the thirty-three channels. It sits at the head of the ENVELOPE group, above DELAY and ATTACK, because it is about the blow rather than about the shape. Zero ignores velocity, which is what the envelope has always done. Positive means a hard note starts sooner and arrives faster, and a soft one waits and then opens slowly. Negative inverts it.

The two halves lean opposite ways on purpose, and each knob below stays the limit of its own row. Velocity only ever lengthens the attack, so ATTACK goes on meaning the fastest that partial gets. Velocity only ever pulls the delay in, so DELAY goes on meaning the latest it ever arrives. Turning STRIKE up cannot outrun either figure, so the rows under it still read honestly at full amount.

Both halves span eight octaves of time, a range of 256 to 1, and it has to be that wide because the attacks worth stretching are short. A partial set to 2 ms attacks in 2 ms under a hard blow and takes half a second under the softest, which is a struck note against a swelled one rather than two kinds of snap. A 400 ms delay stays 400 ms under the softest and comes in to under two milliseconds under the hardest, so a hard blow simply cancels the wait. The stretched attack stops at five seconds, which is as long as the ATTACK row itself goes: a blow can take the onset anywhere the knob could have gone and no further.

Per channel is the point, and it is what separates this from a velocity curve over the whole instrument. Give the upper partials a strike amount and leave the fundamental at zero, and a hard chord lands as one bright block while a soft one unfolds, the fundamental first and the series arriving behind it.

**_Synth Ensemble_ is rebuilt around it.** The patch used to get its ensemble quality from a third of a second of attack on every channel, which meant it played the same way however you struck it. That length now comes from the blow instead: about twenty milliseconds at full velocity, a couple of hundred at half, and close to two seconds at the softest, with the series drifting apart on the way in. Its tremolo is random now rather than a sine, and the noise channel takes a strike amount of its own.

## Removed

**LIFT is gone.** It aimed release velocity at the key-off level, and it was the one control here that most keyboards cannot play: release velocity is rare on hardware, and no factory preset used it. Removing it takes the row back off the strip, so the mixer is exactly as tall as it was before STRIKE was added, and the parameter count is unchanged at 781.

The key-off envelope itself is untouched. SWELL, LEVEL and RELEASE behave exactly as they did, since the figure lift scaled them by was one unless you had dialled it in.

## Fixed

**MPE slide lurched on a controller whose slide axis does not rest at the centre.** Slide arrives as CC74 on the note's own channel, and it was read as an absolute position on that axis with the centre as the rest. That is what a Seaboard sends, and it is not what every controller sends. An Expressive E Osmose splits the key travel in two: the first part sends pressure, and only past a threshold does it begin sending slide, from the bottom of its range upward. Read as a position, the bottom of that range is the far dark end of the slide, so the instant the axis engaged the note jumped abruptly darker and only then began to brighten, which is the opposite of the gesture being made.

Slide is now read as movement rather than as a position. The first value a note is given becomes that note's nought, and what reaches the sound is how far the axis has travelled from it since. A controller that rests at the centre behaves as it always did, since the centre is its first value. One that rests at an end now starts the note on the patch and follows the finger from there.

The travel is scaled so that the longer of the two directions away from the rest is the whole of the slide, and both directions move at that one rate. A note whose rest is the bottom of the axis therefore reaches full slide at the top of it rather than halfway up, and a rest near an end does not turn the sliver of travel behind it into a hair-trigger.

## Upgrading from 1.6.1

Patches, presets and sessions load and sound identical, with one deliberate exception. STRIKE defaults to zero on every channel, so nothing you have already made plays differently. Every factory preset but _Synth Ensemble_ renders to a byte-identical audio file before and after this release, which is the check that says so rather than the claim.

**A patch that used LIFT loses it.** The parameter is no longer there, so a saved session or user preset carrying `hNN_lift` or `noise_lift` loads with those values dropped, and a host automation lane pointed at one finds nothing. That will be nobody on a keyboard that sends no release velocity, which is most of them, but it is a real loss rather than a rename.

**Automation is otherwise unaffected.** There are still 781 parameters: thirty-three `strike` parameters arrived and thirty-three `lift` parameters left. STRIKE is inserted ahead of DELAY rather than appended, but both the Audio Unit and the VST3 identify a parameter by its name rather than its position, so anything already automated stays pointed at the same control.

**A finger that lands forward no longer starts the note bright.** That is the one thing the slide change takes away. Where the note used to take the timbre it was born with as an absolute position, the position a note starts at is now its nought, so landing forward on the pad and holding still is the patch as dialled rather than a brightened version of it. Moving from there does everything it did. The trade buys a slide axis that behaves the same on every controller, which it did not before.

**The factory presets keep their numbering.** None were added or removed, so a session that stored a preset by number opens on the one it saved.

## Installing

The [install page](https://benjamindehli.github.io/overtonium/install/#downloads) has a row per file saying who each one is for. In short: take the installer for your platform, or the zip beside it if you have no administrator rights or keep plugins somewhere of your own.

| Platform | Installer              | Formats                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| macOS    | `.pkg`, with a chooser | VST3, Audio Unit, Standalone |
| Windows  | `.exe`                 | VST3, Standalone             |
| Linux    | none, use the zip      | VST3, LV2, Standalone        |

The macOS build is a universal binary and runs on both Apple Silicon and Intel. Logic and GarageBand only look for new Audio Units when they start, so restart them after installing.

On Linux the zip's contents go in `~/.vst3` and `~/.lv2`.

If you installed an earlier version with the package, the installer replaces it. A plugin folder holding two copies of Overtonium is worth tidying: macOS registers one of them and the host uses whichever that is, which need not be the newer.

## What has actually been tried

All three platforms are built on every push, run through both test suites, and put through pluginval at strictness 8. The VST3 also goes through Steinberg's own conformance validator, and both suites run again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers. macOS is the only platform where the plugin has been loaded into a real host, where the Audio Unit and the VST3 also pass `auval`. Nobody has run the Windows or Linux builds in a DAW yet, so treat those as untried and please open an issue with whatever you find.

The slide fix above is the one thing here that no test could have found. It came back from playing the instrument on an Expressive E Osmose, was traced from a description of how the gesture felt rather than from a failing check, and was confirmed the same way. There is now a test for the shape of it, written after the fact, which is the honest order.

## Windows will warn you

The Windows installer is not signed, so SmartScreen says the publisher is unknown. Choose "More info" and then "Run anyway". If you would rather not, build from source: the readme says how, and it takes one CMake command.

## macOS

The installer is signed and notarised, so it opens without a warning. It asks which of the three formats you want and installs only those.
