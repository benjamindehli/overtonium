# Overtonium 1.17.0

A 32-partial additive synthesizer laid out like a 32-channel mixer. Every channel is one sine oscillator locked to a harmonic of the played note, and every channel has its own tuning, envelope and modulation.

## What is new in 1.17.0

**A velocity curve in decibels (#105).** A dB switch beside the VELOCITY caption picks how velocity becomes level, for every channel at once. On dB the row takes its reach off in decibels, up to 40 dB on the softest note, so equal steps of the knob and of velocity are equal steps of loudness. Classic is the old curve, which is linear in amplitude, so at full a medium note was only 6 dB down. Sessions and preset files from before the switch load on Classic and sound as they did. Clicking the switch converts every channel's amount so a medium note keeps its level, in one undo step. The VELOCITY row now starts at zero.

**A KEYBOARD row in OUTPUT (#106).** It voices a partial across the keyboard, the way an organ builder voices a stop. It only ever turns keys down, so the fader stays the loudest the partial gets. Up to 100% either way it tilts around middle C by up to 24 dB per octave, with the gentle slopes given most of the knob. Past 100% the slope holds and the turn moves away from middle C, an octave for every 20%, so a partial can sound on only part of the keyboard. Its readout shows the slope and the key it turns on. It reads the key, not the pitch, so bends and the tuning leave the level alone, and under a glide it slides with the pitch. The OUTPUT rule carries a new lamp in three parts for the rows under it.

**The sostenuto pedal (#118).** CC 66 holds the notes whose keys are down when it is pressed, as MIDI defines it, and lets the sustain pedal take them over if it is down when the sostenuto pedal comes up. It works under MPE, each zone with its own pedals. A SOSTENUTO lamp sits under SUSTAIN on the Playing bar. In Piano mode the resonator keeps the strings of the held notes free.

**Half-pedalling (#119).** The sustain pedal is read as a depth. A continuous pedal part way down lets go of notes slowly, the further up the faster, and never holds a note longer than all the way down or stops it sooner than all the way up. An on/off pedal does exactly what it did. In Piano mode the resonator's dampers rest on the strings as far as the pedal is up.

**The resonator follows STRETCH (#120).** With STRETCH on, the resonator's strings are as stiff as the partials, so their overtones are spread the same way and a stretched partial still rings its string. At Metallic Piano's 24 cents, the strings had stopped hearing the upper half of the series. A patch without STRETCH sounds and costs exactly what it did.

**The resonator rings in stereo (#121).** Each string rings back on the side of the partials that drive it, so a panned patch keeps its resonator with it. A patch with every partial in the middle rings in the middle, exactly as before.

**Two new resonator modes (#122).** **Fifths** is a drone of the tuning root and the pure fifth above it in four octaves, from C2 to G5 on a root of C, always free. **Chord** is Piano that keeps the last chord: the strings of keys struck together stay free after you let go, so a line played over the chord leaves it ringing. A key added while the chord is held joins it, and a chord struck after letting go replaces it.

**Solo or mute only one channel (#101).** Option-click, or Alt-click on Windows and Linux, on M or S mutes or solos that channel and turns the switch off on every other. The right-click menu on M and S has the same as an item.

**Set every channel to any shape (#103).** The PITCH MOD and AMP MOD shape menus have **Set every channel to** with every shape in it, beside **Set every channel to this**.

**175% and 200% zoom (#104).** The Zoom menu in Settings goes up to twice the size.

**Help on the row names (#102).** Hold the pointer over a row's name in the gutter to see what the row does.

**Three new presets.** _Chapel_ is a small pipe organ on PD, its pipes wavering on their wind, into the room and the resonator's open strings. _Pendulum_ gives every partial its own tremolo and pitch movement, faster the higher it sits, so the movement slows as a note rings, on 6581. _Soft Trio_ is a Karplus-Strong string from the noise channel over a vibraphone brought in by pressure or the mod wheel, with a digital echo answering on the other side. Forty-four presets in all.

**The factory presets are on the dB curve.** Each was moved over by one rule, which keeps the hardest notes as they were and gives the softer ones more range. _8-bit Choir_, _FM Piano_, _Metallic Piano_, _Synth Ensemble_, _Tape Pluck_ and _Wurli_ were then tuned again by ear, mostly giving the upper partials more velocity and using the KEYBOARD row.

**A quieter panel.** The level meters, the output meter and the macro faders sit in recesses like the lamps. The meters are tighter, the knob rings show their unlit ticks in the knob's own colour, and the faders have glass caps. The colour bars over the channel numbers are gone, which makes the header row shorter, and the noise channel is marked with a noise trace instead of NZ.

## Fixed

**The noise channel's level readout stayed lit under another channel's solo.** It now goes dark and comes back with the rest of the strip, as every channel's readouts do.

**Switching from Pure or Bulb to another character dropped the sound out for a moment.** Those two leave the bus drive's curve unused, and the next character started its filters from where they had stopped. They now keep going while no curve uses them.

**Two bounces of the same passage could differ.** The hiss, the flutter and the other random movement now start again from the same point whenever the host resets the plugin, so an offline bounce comes out the same every time.

**A key let go in legato could come back.** A key pressed in mono legato and released after switching out of it counted as held when legato came back on. A panic, a reset or a change of mode now forgets every held key.

**Bind a different controller added one instead of replacing it.** MIDI Learn now gives a control the controller it learns and lets go of the one it had. The channel mode messages, CC 120 to 127, can no longer be learned, since some hosts send reset all controllers when playback stops.

**The tail could be cut short.** The tail the host is told about now counts a macro on RELEASE or SWELL, so a bounce does not end before the notes do.

**A session file could silence the plugin.** 1.16.0 skipped values that are not numbers in a preset file. Now a session does the same.

**Undo could reach back past a restored session.** Loading a session now starts a new undo history. Closing the window in the middle of a drag no longer leaves every later edit out of the history.

**Two ways the window could crash the host.** Answering a notice with something that opened another notice, and closing the window while one of the bar's menus was open. Both now check that what they answer to is still there.

**The limiter would have lost its place after about a day of sound.** The lookahead's positions now stay inside their ring however long the plugin runs.

## Under it

**884 parameters.** The velocity curve and the 33 KEYBOARD rows went on the end of the list, so automation written before them keeps its place.

**Shared with the other Dehli Musikk plugins.** The output stage and its eight machines, the look and feel and the controls now come from dehli-musikk-plugin-core, a library shared with the new Console and Cassette plugins, so all three run the same code. It is pinned by commit and checked against its hash, as JUCE is. Every clip and every picture on the site came out the same after the move.

**JUCE 9.0.5.**

**The site's clips are checked on every build.** The Linux build renders every clip and compares it with the one on the site, so a change that moves the sound cannot leave the site out of date.

**A cleanup.** Code that was no longer reached is gone, code that was written more than once is shared, and the tests share their helpers. The effects now share one filter coefficient, worked out in double precision, which moved 26 clips by one step of 16-bit audio. Every other change was checked by hashing every sample of 86 setups before and after.

**A sweep of 3124 cases before release.** 1.16.0's cases with the new strings among the setups (Fifths, Chord, stretched up and down, and stereo), the KEYBOARD row pushed to both ends, and the three velocity curves. The sustain pedal was held halfway and the sostenuto pedal caught a chord under the playing, the resonator was switched through all seven modes every 50 ms under ringing notes, and all forty-four presets went through eight ways of playing. Every case renders finite, bounded audio, every case meant to sound makes sound, and the whole sweep ran again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers.

## Upgrading from 1.16.0

**Automation of the resonator's mode and the macros' rows lands differently.** A host stores a choice as a fraction of the list's length. The resonator's mode list grows from five choices to seven, and each macro's row list from twenty to twenty-one, so a lane drawn before 1.17.0 lands a little further along. Sessions and presets are unaffected, since Fifths, Chord and Keyboard went on the end.

**MIDI program changes from 8 up land later.** _Chapel_ sorts at program 8, _Pendulum_ at 29 and _Soft Trio_ at 32, so 1.16.0's programs 8 to 27 move up one, 28 and 29 move up two, and 30 to 40 move up three. A saved session stores the preset's name as well as its number and is unaffected.

**The factory presets play softer notes differently.** They are on the dB curve, so a soft note is quieter against a hard one than before. The hardest notes are as loud as they were, apart from the six that were tuned again by ear. Your own sessions and presets stay on Classic until you click the switch.

**The new rows start at nothing.** A session or preset file from before 1.17.0 loads the KEYBOARD rows at zero and the velocity curve on Classic, so it sounds as it did.

## Installing

The [install page](https://benjamindehli.github.io/overtonium/install/#downloads) has a row per file saying who each one is for. In short: take the installer for your platform, or the zip beside it if you have no administrator rights or keep plugins somewhere of your own.

| Platform | Installer              | Formats                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| macOS    | `.pkg`, with a chooser | VST3, Audio Unit, Standalone |
| Windows  | `.exe`                 | VST3, Standalone             |
| Linux    | none, use the zip      | VST3, LV2, Standalone        |
