# Overtonium 1.11.0

A 32-partial additive synthesiser laid out like a 32-channel mixer. Every channel is one sine oscillator locked to a harmonic of the played note, and every channel has its own tuning, envelope and modulation.

## What is new in 1.11.0

**Eight macros, so a relationship across the series can be automated.** This is what issue #24 asked for, and it asked for the right thing: a LINK drag across the tuning knobs moves 32 parameters and a host catches only the last one touched, so a relationship the instrument lets you edit by hand could not be recorded at all. A macro gives that relationship one control, and a host one lane.

A macro names a row, a scope and a curve, and then one knob drives every channel the scope reaches. The scope is the same set LINK offers, All, Odd, Even and the twelve intervals, and the curve shares LINK's arithmetic rather than reimplementing it. Where LINK measures its taper from the strip under the mouse, a macro has no mouse, so it names an anchor channel to lean from.

**It offsets rather than writes**, which is the decision the whole feature turns on. The macro lays its amount over what the patch says on the way to the engine, so the patch underneath is untouched and the host has one parameter instead of thirty-two moves. Writing would have reproduced the very problem the issue reports.

The visible consequence is that the knobs do not move when a macro does, which is normal for anything that works this way and surprising the first time. So a driven control says both things at once: the ring of ticks lights to where the macro has taken the value, in the macro's colour, and the pointer on the cap stays at what the patch holds, in the channel's. The gap between them is the macro. At rest the two coincide, so a macro that has been made but not moved looks like no macro at all. A fader says it in the gutters either side of its track, which grow two narrow columns of the meter's own segments and light from the foot up, and the glass of the cap takes a wash of the same colour.

The amount is signed and spans the whole of the row it drives, so a macro on LEVEL can duck a group as well as lift it, and one knob can reach a channel wherever it happens to be set. The cost is that a control already at an end of its range has a dead stretch of macro fader in that direction, which is the price of the reach rather than a fault.

Eight is a pool rather than a count. A plugin declares its parameters once and neither VST3 nor AU can add one later, so making a macro takes one of eight that already exist and removing it puts that one back. A macro whose row is None is one nobody has made.

**MIDI Learn.** Right-click any control, choose MIDI Learn, and move something on the controller. The control rings in a colour of its own while it waits, and the window gives that ring back as soon as a message arrives.

The map is one parameter per controller number. A knob can be reached from more than one controller and a controller only ever moves one knob, because the other way round needs a rule for what happens when two knobs disagree about where a controller is standing.

Eleven controller numbers are refused rather than bound: the mod wheel, the sustain pedal, the MPE slide axis, the two that stop notes, and the six a keyboard declares its zones with. Binding any of those would leave the instrument unable to be told what it is plugged into, and the slide is the one most easily taken by accident, since it moves whenever a finger does. Refusing is silent in the useful sense, so arming a knob and then reaching for the slide to steady a note leaves the note steadied and the knob still waiting.

Bindings are saved with the session and never with a patch, alongside the window size and the fold state, because which controller moves which knob describes the desk you are sitting at rather than the sound. A binding records the parameter's name rather than its position, so a later version adding controls cannot repoint your controller at something else.

**CLIP is five machines instead of a switch.** It began as one soft clipper that existed so that pushing 32 faders up could not hand the host something louder than it asked for. It is part of the sound now, which is also why the master fader in front of it travels with a preset.

_Soft_ is the curve that was already there, and it came through the change bit for bit, compared against the old formula written out longhand rather than against the code, so nothing dialled in against it has moved. _Hard_ is a wall at unity: nothing happens until it does, which is a converter with no headroom left. _Asymmetric_ lets one half of the wave lean over before the other, the way a single-ended stage runs out of supply in one direction first, and that gives even harmonics where Soft and Hard give odd ones. Measured against Soft at the same drive, its second harmonic is a hundred times larger. _Limiter_ does not bend the wave at all: it turns the level down as the signal approaches the ceiling and lets it back up afterwards, so what you hear is the level moving rather than the shape, and it is the only one here a mastering engineer would recognise. _Fold_ turns the wave back on itself past the threshold instead of flattening it, which makes far more harmonics than the others and moves them with the level, so it sings where the rest crunch.

Every one of the five is bounded by full scale. That is the point of the stage rather than a property of the curves that happened to be chosen, and it is tested: each is held to it at drives up to 400 times, on its own and through the whole instrument, at three sample rates.

**The limiter looks ahead, which is why the plugin now reports more latency.** Without a window the gain only starts moving once the peak has arrived, so what actually held the signal down was the hard clip behind it. On the presets that drive it hardest, two seconds of audio reached 1.294 and had 1741 samples cut off. A limiter whose work is done by a clipper is a clipper.

Two milliseconds, and the whole gain path fits inside it. Every window size tried removed the clipping, so the choice was how gently the gain moves against how much latency to charge for it:

| Lookahead | Peak before the backstop | Samples clipped | Gain movement |
| --------- | ------------------------ | --------------- | ------------- |
| none      | 1.294                    | 1741            | 0.0041        |
| 1 ms      | 0.980                    | 0               | 0.0040        |
| 2 ms      | 0.981                    | 0               | 0.0020        |
| 5 ms      | 0.983                    | 0               | 0.0007        |

Five milliseconds was the first thought and would have bought a curve two and a half times gentler still, which is below where it can be heard, for three more milliseconds of delay.

Every shape pays it and so does the stage switched off, because a latency that depended on the patch would have the host re-plan its graph on every preset load. The Settings menu can refuse the lookahead, and that is the one thing allowed to move the figure, since a session setting is not something a preset or the bar can reach. It is worth having because only one of the five uses the window: if you never reach for the Limiter you are paying two milliseconds for nothing. What you lose by refusing it is the smoothing rather than the ceiling.

**The parameters scroll.** Issue #5: the rows are small on a 1080p screen, and zooming in far enough to read them made the window taller than such a screen has room for.

A channel is three bands now. The channel number at the top is pinned, the mute, the solo and the fader at the foot are pinned, and everything between them scrolls behind an opening. The number and the fader are the two things you find your place by on a desk, so neither is ever what has scrolled away. Above the height where it all fits this is exactly the layout that was there before, with no scrolling and the fader taking the slack. Below it the fader keeps the height it wants and the rows move instead, because the fader being squeezed off the bottom was the complaint.

One scroll position for the whole mixer rather than one per column, since the captions in the gutter name the knobs beside them and two columns a row apart would be captions pointing at the wrong controls. The wheel scrolls it, there is a scrollbar at the far right when there is anything to scroll, and a trackpad's horizontal gesture moves the series sideways.

**The panel was rebuilt.** Every switch is a lit plastic cap sitting in a moulded well, one white translucent cap with the lamp shining through it and a fixed near-black legend printed on the plastic, so a word does not change colour under its own lamp. The mute and the solo are a two-gang block sharing one moulding. The knobs are discs with an eight-slot collar that turns with the value, and the notches are cut out of the collar rather than painted on it, so what shows between two flutes is the panel behind and the shadow the cap throws onto it.

The preset name is spelled on a fourteen-bar display fitted to nine cells, which is the same kind of display the tuning and level figures already used. The readouts' segments were reshaped, their gaps tightened, and the leading cell now holds either a sign or a hundreds digit so that nothing in a reading shuffles sideways when the number goes negative.

Every partial is on one grey. The octaves and the noise channel used to take a shade up from the rest, and both had to go for the same reason: the hover highlight is a wash of much the same weight, so a mixer carrying either of them had two kinds of lightened channel and no way to tell which was which without counting.

**The OUTPUT rule carries two lamps**, so all five rules carry something. The left one is VELOCITY and the right one AFTERTOUCH, and each reads only its own row on that channel. The velocity lamp is the gain velocity has left the partial, so it is full when the blow has cost that partial nothing and dark when the row has taken all of it. The aftertouch lamp is the amount against the pressure, and rests dark.

**One tool rather than two switches.** LINK and DRAW were never independent, since a drag cannot be a link and a drawing at once, and the two switches worked around that by showing LINK as off while drawing was armed without it being off. One button chooses between Pointer, Link and Draw, and LINK's own settings sit under the three in the same menu. The button wears the pointer it gives rather than a word.

**LINK says which channels it would reach.** Choosing a scope lights the channel number at the head of every strip the drag would move, graded by the curve, so the answer is on the mixer rather than in your head. All lights all of them, which is the honest answer to that scope rather than an exception to the rule.

**A thirty-third factory preset.** _6581 Triangle_ is the triangle of a MOS 6581, the SID in a Commodore 64. A triangle is odd harmonics falling as the square of their number and this is not quite that: the chip folds the top bit of an eight-bit accumulator and drops the lowest, so the ramp it draws is a staircase of 2048 steps rather than a line. What that leaves is a triangle carrying even harmonics it should not have and a noise floor a mathematical one does not have, which is most of why a SID sounds like a SID. The level sits low and the clipper is on because the chip's own output stage distorts, and that is the other half of the sound.

**A preset carries the master fader and the clipper.** Both used to be session settings that no preset could reach, which was right while the clipper was only a guard and wrong once it became five machines. All 33 factory presets have been levelled by hand and had their stage chosen.

**There is an accessibility statement.** `ACCESSIBILITY.md` says what is checked rather than intended, with the measurements behind it, and says plainly what is not there: the plugin's own window is not keyboard operable, which is the largest barrier in the project, and nothing has been evaluated by anyone who relies on assistive technology. Every control carries a distinct spoken name and every control is a host parameter, so a host's own generic editor is a complete alternative to the mixer rather than a reduced one.

## Fixed

**A partial silenced by a negative AFTERTOUCH amount took every lamp on its channel dark with it.** The voices are gathered by taking the loudest reading for each partial, and the comparison was being read as a threshold, so a partial sitting at exactly zero was never claimed by any voice and its whole channel went unreported. The envelope and tremolo lamps had always been wrong in the same way and nobody had found a patch that showed it.

**A note played while the channel was already under pressure thumped or faded in.** The pressure smoother was reset to nothing at every note-on, so it spent fifteen milliseconds travelling to a level that was already dialled in: a thump with a negative amount, a fade in with a positive one. It now starts where the channel is holding it. Per-note pressure still starts at zero, which is correct.

That is the whole of this section. Plenty else went wrong on the way here, but all of it was in work that had not shipped, so none of it is a fix to anything you have been using.

## Under it

**Fifty parameters were added, 836 in all.** Forty-eight of them are the eight macros, six each, and the other two are the clip type and whether the limiter looks ahead. All fifty went on the end, so every automation lane written before this release still points at the control it was pointing at.

**JUCE 9.0.3**, and the WebP decoder it vendors is switched off. JUCE registers that decoder in a static format list the linker cannot see past, so it ships whether or not anything decodes a WebP, and nothing here does. Turning it off takes 717 KB off an arm64 plugin binary, about a tenth of it, and roughly 1.4 MB off the universal macOS one.

**The editor's session writes and the host's saves now hold one lock.** The window keeps nine things on the state tree that are not parameters, the window size and zoom, the scroll, the fold mask and which tool is selected, and it wrote them straight onto the tree from the message thread while a host may ask for that same tree from another. A value tree is not thread safe and the lock inside JUCE's parameter state does not cover a write from outside it, so the two walked the same reference-counted graph at once, which corrupts a refcount and therefore the heap, somewhere else, later. Take the lock out again and the thread sanitiser reports fourteen races there, every one of them on a reference count inside the tree. It was the same tool that found a fifteenth, on the program number stored beside the tree and written by the same two methods. With both fixed it reports none.

**The macro offset moves through a control's travel rather than its units**, which is a trap worth writing down. Several rows are built with a logarithmic range that carries its curve in conversion functions and leaves the skew at 1, so asking the skew whether a row is linear answers the wrong question, and rebuilding a range from its two endpoints throws the curve away silently. That is what made a quarter turn of a macro add five seconds to a decay.

## Upgrading from 1.10.1

**The plugin reports 108 samples of latency at 48 kHz**, where it reported 12. The 96 are the limiter's two-millisecond window, and every clip shape pays them, including the stage switched off. Settings can refuse the lookahead and take the figure back to 12. Most hosts re-plan their graph when the figure changes, some only notice on playback stop or on reload, so it is a setting to choose and leave rather than to ride.

**Loading a preset now moves the master fader and the clipper.** Both left the set of session settings that presets cannot reach. A session you saved is unaffected, since it carries every value it stored, but choosing a preset now sets your output level where it used to leave it alone.

**A MIDI program change may name a different preset.** Factory presets are numbered by their position in the alphabetical list and _6581 Triangle_ sorts third, so every preset from _Big Saw_ onwards has moved up by one. A clip or a foot controller sending a number will pick the sound above the one it used to. Sessions are not affected: a saved session carries the preset's name as well as its index, and the name is what decides.

**Automation is unaffected.** The fifty new parameters went on the end and nothing else moved.

**Your own patches get Soft and no macros**, which is what every patch written before this had, because the clip type and the macro rows are new parameters and an old patch says nothing about them.

## Installing

The [install page](https://benjamindehli.github.io/overtonium/install/#downloads) has a row per file saying who each one is for. In short: take the installer for your platform, or the zip beside it if you have no administrator rights or keep plugins somewhere of your own.

| Platform | Installer              | Formats                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| macOS    | `.pkg`, with a chooser | VST3, Audio Unit, Standalone |
| Windows  | `.exe`                 | VST3, Standalone             |
| Linux    | none, use the zip      | VST3, LV2, Standalone        |

The macOS build is a universal binary and runs on both Apple Silicon and Intel. Logic and GarageBand only look for new Audio Units when they start, so restart them after installing.

On Linux the zip's contents go in `~/.vst3` and `~/.lv2`.

If you installed an earlier version with the package, the installer replaces it. A plugin folder holding two copies of Overtonium is worth tidying: macOS registers one of them and the host uses whichever that is, which need not be the newer.

## What has actually been tried

All three platforms are built on every push, run through both test suites, and put through pluginval at strictness 8. The VST3 also goes through Steinberg's own conformance validator, and both suites run again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers. macOS is the only platform where the plugin has been loaded into a real host, where the Audio Unit and the VST3 also pass `auval`. Nobody has run the Windows or Linux builds in a DAW yet, so treat those as untried and please open an issue with whatever you find.

The panel was rebuilt against photographs of real hardware, measured rather than described. Where a reference decided a proportion, that proportion is in the source with the measurement beside it, along with what was tried before it and why that was wrong.

Both faults above came back from playing the instrument rather than from a test, and the tests were written afterwards, which is the honest order. The lamp one is worth naming twice: it had been wrong for the envelope and tremolo lamps since those lamps existed, and it passed every test it had, because the case it needed was a partial sitting at exactly zero and nothing had asked for one.

Nothing in `ACCESSIBILITY.md` has been checked by someone who uses a screen reader or a keyboard to work. The names are verified programmatically, which proves they exist and are distinct and proves nothing about whether they are useful to listen to.

## Windows will warn you

The Windows installer is not signed, so SmartScreen says the publisher is unknown. Choose "More info" and then "Run anyway". If you would rather not, build from source: the readme says how, and it takes one CMake command.

## macOS

The installer is signed and notarised, so it opens without a warning. It asks which of the three formats you want and installs only those.
