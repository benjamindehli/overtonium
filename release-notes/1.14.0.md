# Overtonium 1.14.0

A 32-partial additive synthesizer laid out like a 32-channel mixer. Every channel is one sine oscillator locked to a harmonic of the played note, and every channel has its own tuning, envelope and modulation.

## What is new in 1.14.0

**A string in the noise channel.** Issue #54 asked for Karplus-Strong synthesis. The noise channel now has a string to strike: a delay line one period of the note long, fed back through a gentle low-pass filter, so a burst of noise sent into it comes out as a plucked note that loses its high frequencies first. It sits in the noise strip under COLOUR, with four knobs. **STRING** crossfades from the noise alone to the string alone. **DECAY** sets how long it rings while the key is down, from 50 ms to 10 s. **DAMP** sets how quickly it loses its high frequencies. **POINT** sets where along the string the noise strikes, from near the end, which keeps every harmonic, to the middle, which removes the even ones.

The noise channel's own envelope decides how the string is played, so there is no mode switch. A noise envelope a few milliseconds long plucks the string and lets it ring, like a harpsichord or a guitar under the organ. A noise held on a short, dark string blows into it, which gives a pitched breath. The string is tuned to the note and follows the pitch wheel, glide, MPE, every temperament and Adaptive's slides, the way the fundamental does. RELEASE damps it after the key comes up. At the top of the keyboard, where the string is too short to tune, it fades back to plain noise instead of playing out of tune.

**The resonator.** Issue #53 asked for sympathetic strings. The resonator is eighty-eight of them, one for each key of a piano from A0 to C8, that ring with what you play. They are fed the sum of the thirty-three channels and mixed back in before the bus drive, so the character drives them with everything else and they sound like part of the instrument, not a room around it. It has its own frame on the top bar, after the character's, with a button for the mode and three knobs.

**Piano** works like a piano. The strings of the keys you hold are free, the rest are damped, and the sustain pedal lifts every damper, so the pedal adds a bloom under a chord. **Open** is Piano with the pedal always down. **Octave** is twelve strings from C3, always free, so every note finds something to ring with. **Series** is the root's first eight harmonics, tuned pure, which works as a drone that answers any note on the root's own harmonic series. The strings follow the temperament, the root and the reference pitch, and under Adaptive a string waits at the pitch its key last had.

**MIX** goes from the instrument alone to the resonator alone, and the level stays about the same as you turn it up. **DECAY** sets how long a free string rings, from 200 ms to 10 s, and the level stays about the same at any setting, with the pedal up or down. **DAMP** sets how quickly the high end dies away. A damper stops its string over 0.8 s at the bottom of the keyboard and 0.25 s at the top, so a released bass key leaves the short hum of a felt damper on a heavy string.

**Space Cowboy.** A new preset that uses both: a synthetic tremolo guitar plucked from the noise channel's string, over a pad with square-wave pitch modulation in some of its partials and a whistle like a theremin that glides between notes, with the resonator in Open under all of it. **Metallic Piano** now uses the resonator in Piano mode. Thirty-five presets in all.

**A rearranged top bar.** SETTINGS, MACROS, PLAYING and TUNING come first, as four icons in two rows, then the wordmark over the preset screen. The tagline is gone. The bar is evened out, with the same space at both ends, between the four buttons and around the frames, and it fits on one row from 1285 px, on two from 675.

**The update question is asked in the window.** The first time a window opens, a notice inside it asks whether to check for new versions. It stays until you answer, so a host scanning its plugins does not use it up. With the check on, a new version is announced once in the same way, and SETTINGS stays lit until you install it, with the release as the first entry in its menu.

**Lights that are off are dark.** A muted channel, or one left out by a solo, turns its knob rings, readouts and the macro columns beside its fader fully off, instead of grey. So do the knobs of the resonator, the echo and the reverb while they are off. The pointer on each knob and the fader cap still show where they are set.

**A faster mixer.** Soloing a channel, scrolling the mixer and resizing the window redraw every strip, and in measurements they now take about a fifth of the time they did. The knobs, faders, buttons and readouts are drawn from pictures of themselves when nothing about them has changed, and the 32 strips share one picture of their background.

**Shorter tooltips.** The tooltips on the panel say what a control does in a sentence or two, in plainer words.

## Fixed

**Turning a knob in the bars with the mouse wheel scrolled the mixer too.** Issue #58. A wheel over a knob on the top bar, the Playing bar or the Tuning bar now turns the knob and nothing else, as it already did in the mixer.

**Fit all 32 channels left the faders a few pixels short.** It left out the strips' own padding and kept room for a sideways scrollbar that the window at that width does not have. It now gives every row its full height.

**A possible crash in Windows hosts when a window closes.** JUCE 9.0.4 fixes an accessibility handler that could outlive the window it belonged to and be read after the window was gone, which could crash a host on Windows while a screen reader or another accessibility tool was running. It may be what was behind a reported crash in FL Studio when Overtonium was removed or the project closed.

## Under it

**850 parameters.** The four for the noise channel's string and the four for the resonator went on the end, so every parameter keeps the position it had for a host that goes by position. All eight belong to the patch. A preset that does not set them has the string at zero and the resonator off, and renders bit for bit as it did in 1.13.0.

**What they cost.** The string is one delay line per voice. The resonator costs about a tenth of what eight voices do in Piano or Octave, and about half in Open or with the pedal down in Piano, when all eighty-eight strings are free. A string that has gone quiet behind its damper costs nothing. The resonator's levels slide rather than step when its mode or MIX changes, and switched off it fades out and is stopped, so it is not there at all.

**JUCE 9.0.4**, a bug-fix release. Nothing in the plugin's own code had to change for it.

**A sweep of 832 cases before release.** 1.13.0's 456, with the string and the resonator added at every one of eight sample rates from 8 kHz to 192 kHz and five block sizes from 1 to 4096 samples, including the resonator switched between modes, its MIX moved and the pedal going down and up while it rings. Then every string and resonator setup against six ways of playing on four presets and at both ends of the rate range. Every case renders finite, bounded audio and actually makes sound, and the whole sweep ran again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers.

## Upgrading from 1.13.0

**MIDI program changes from 25 up land one preset later.** Space Cowboy sorts at program 25, so Space Flute through Wurli move from 25 to 33 up to 26 to 34. A saved session stores the preset's name as well as its number and is unaffected.

**Metallic Piano sounds different.** It now rings its own strings, so a session that uses it will sound fuller, especially with the pedal down. Its level was lowered to match.

**The top bar's buttons moved** to the left end, with the wordmark beside them. Nothing on it was removed.

## Installing

The [install page](https://benjamindehli.github.io/overtonium/install/#downloads) has a row per file saying who each one is for. In short: take the installer for your platform, or the zip beside it if you have no administrator rights or keep plugins somewhere of your own.

| Platform | Installer              | Formats                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| macOS    | `.pkg`, with a chooser | VST3, Audio Unit, Standalone |
| Windows  | `.exe`                 | VST3, Standalone             |
| Linux    | none, use the zip      | VST3, LV2, Standalone        |

The macOS build is a universal binary and runs on both Apple Silicon and Intel. Logic and GarageBand only look for new Audio Units when they start, so restart them after installing.

On Linux the zip's contents go in `~/.vst3` and `~/.lv2`.

If you installed an earlier version with the package, the installer replaces it.

## What has actually been tried

All three platforms are built on every push, run through both test suites, and put through pluginval at strictness 8. The VST3 also goes through Steinberg's own conformance validator, and both suites run again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers. The string and the resonator were played in a real host on macOS, where the Audio Unit and the VST3 also pass `auval`. Nobody has run the Windows or Linux builds in a DAW yet, so treat those as untried and please open an issue with whatever you find.
