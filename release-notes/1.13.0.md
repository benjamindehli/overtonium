# Overtonium 1.13.0

A 32-partial additive synthesizer laid out like a 32-channel mixer. Every channel is one sine oscillator locked to a harmonic of the played note, and every channel has its own tuning, envelope and modulation.

## What is new in 1.13.0

**Adaptive, a temperament that retunes as you play.** Issue #45 asked for adaptive just intonation after Eivind Groven, whose organs gave every key more than one pitch and chose between them as they were played. Every fixed temperament is in tune somewhere and out of tune somewhere else: just major on C plays I–IV–V–I in G with a chord a whole comma out. Adaptive gives every pitch class five versions, its place in just major on the root and one and two commas either side, and keeps the chord you are holding on whichever versions make it purest.

The chord is settled again every time a key goes down or comes up, so a chord turning into the next a note at a time stays in tune all the way, not only one struck fresh. It moves as few notes as it can. A held note that the new chord needs somewhere else slides there over the **Retune** time, from a jump at zero to a second, 30 ms to begin with. The keys you hold decide the tuning, not the order they went down in, so a chord struck at once, played a key at a time or reached from another chord comes out the same, interval for interval. A ringing chord settles what the held keys leave open, and a key struck into silence takes the version its pitch class used last.

Like Groven's organs it tempers each fifth by an eighth of a schisma, a quarter of a cent, so the major thirds stay pure and chords far from the root are not left two cents out. Where a chord cannot be pure all the way through, as in C11, it gives up as little as it can, a fifth or a fourth counting for more than a third and a third for more than a seventh.

**The harmonic seventh.** A minor seventh is tuned 7:4 above its root, 969 cents, so a dominant seventh becomes 4:5:6:7, the seventh every partial already carries as its seventh harmonic. It is noticeably lower than the equal-tempered seventh, which is most of why it sounds so settled. Where another held note would then be out of tune with it, a minor third above the root as in a minor seventh chord for one, the seventh keeps 9:5. The **7:4** switch, Harmonic sevenths in Settings, turns it off.

**No drift.** Left to itself, a progression tuned pure chord to chord carries the keyboard off by commas, and Adaptive lets it go up to two either way before it holds. The **NO DRIFT** switch, No drift in Settings, keeps the chord as near home as it can instead: a progression that would carry it off moves a held note a comma, and a key struck into silence goes home. Played legato twice round C, A minor, D minor and G, C ends two commas low with it off and exactly where it began with it on.

**Two bars under the top bar.** SETTINGS and MACROS stand over two new buttons, PLAYING, a keyboard, and TUNING, a tuning fork, each opening a bar between the top bar and the mixer. Both start closed, so nothing changes until you open one, and each instance remembers which are open. A bar takes its height from the mixer, which scrolls, so the window keeps its size. In a narrow window a bar wraps onto further rows the way the top bar does. Every setting in a bar opens the same list as its entry in Settings, which keeps every entry.

**The Tuning bar** holds the temperament, root and reference pitch, and under Adaptive the drift, NO DRIFT, 7:4, a **PURE** lamp and the Retune knob. Last come twelve displays, one for each note, saying how far it sits from equal temperament in cents, with the white keys on a lighter ground the way a keyboard has them. Under Adaptive the notes you hold light and show where they are sounding, PURE lights while every interval in the chord is pure, 7:4 while a seventh is on the harmonic seventh, and DRIFT says how far the keyboard has gone, in commas. Under every other temperament the twelve chart the scale, so you can see what Werckmeister III or just on D does to each note before you play it.

**The Playing bar** holds the voice count, Legato, No stack, the bend range, MPE, Aftertouch from and Slide to, and shows what your hands are doing. **IN USE** counts the voices sounding, release tails included, so it reaches the voice count just as a new note has to take one over. **SUSTAIN** lights while a pedal is down. **PITCH** shows the pitch wheel from the middle, or under MPE the key bent furthest, and **WHEEL** and **PRESSURE** rise from the left, PRESSURE showing the keyboard's own pressure whatever Aftertouch from passes on.

**The macros as knobs.** The Playing bar ends with the eight macros, each a knob in its colour captioned with the row it is aimed at, turning the same amount as its slider in MACROS. A macro not yet made shows a blank knob with its number. Right-click a knob to assign its macro, to change it in MACROS, or to bind a controller to it.

**Segment displays that behave like real ones.** Every display has a fixed row of cells, each with its bars and a decimal point, and a reading lights some of them and leaves the rest dark. Nothing is drawn as a dimmed figure any more. Letters read cleanly at the size names are drawn, a sharp is a sign beside the root's cell rather than a broken letter, and every display in the bars is as wide as its cells need.

**No stack.** One voice per key is renamed for what it does: a repeated note does not stack on its own tail. Its cap, Settings entry and host parameter all say No stack.

## Fixed

**Right-clicking a knob on the top bar never offered MIDI Learn.** The top bar never asked to hear clicks on its children, so a right-click went to the knob alone and the menu never opened. It does now, and so does every knob in the two bars.

**A host still called the shared modulator switches "In Phase".** 1.12.0 renamed them Shared by all notes on the panel and missed their host parameter names, which are now Pitch Mod Shared and Amp Mod Shared.

## Under it

**842 parameters.** Adaptive Retune, Adaptive Harmonic Sevenths and Adaptive No Drift went on the end, so every parameter keeps the position it had for a host that goes by position. All three belong to the session, like the temperament, so loading a preset never changes them.

**What Adaptive costs.** The chord is worked out on a key going down or up, not on every sample, and takes about 28 microseconds with sixteen keys held. Under every other temperament the engine does what it did: every factory preset and every clip on the site renders bit for bit as it did in 1.12.0.

**Tested against every chord type in music-utils.** The 125 chord types of [music-utils](https://github.com/benjamindehli/music-utils), a library for naming chords from the notes held, from a bare third to an altered thirteenth, are played through Adaptive in all twelve keys, struck at once, arpeggiated and reached from another chord, on every build. Each has to come out the same however it is played, and as pure as a search of every possible tuning allows.

**The readouts are handed over without a lock.** What Adaptive did to the held chord goes from the audio thread to the window through a triple buffer, and the Playing bar's readouts as single values, so the audio thread never waits for the window. Both were checked under the thread sanitizer.

**A sweep of 456 cases before release.** 1.12.0's 310, with Adaptive added at every one of eight sample rates from 8 kHz to 192 kHz and five block sizes from 1 to 4096 samples: a lone A on a root of A sounding exactly 440 Hz, and the top line with five-second glides and one-second retunes. Then three Adaptive setups against five ways of playing on four presets and at both ends of the rate range. Every case renders finite, bounded audio and actually makes sound.

## Upgrading from 1.12.0

**Temperament automation lands differently.** Adaptive is a seventh entry on the end of the temperament list, and a host automates a choice by its position along the list, so a lane drawn against six entries now lands a little further along. A saved session stores the temperament itself and is unaffected.

**A host shows new names** for three parameters: No Stack, formerly One Voice Per Key, and Pitch Mod Shared and Amp Mod Shared, formerly Pitch Mod In Phase and Amp Mod In Phase. Their ids are unchanged, so automation and sessions still reach them.

**The window can grow** when a bar opens in a window already as short as it goes, since the window's minimum height counts the open bars. Otherwise a bar takes its height from the mixer.

**The converter readouts no longer dim.** They say what the rate and depth are, not whether a cut is active, and a channel's 0.0, Et and -inF read lit, since a segment is lit or it is not.

## Installing

The [install page](https://benjamindehli.github.io/overtonium/install/#downloads) has a row per file saying who each one is for. In short: take the installer for your platform, or the zip beside it if you have no administrator rights or keep plugins somewhere of your own.

| Platform | Installer              | Formats                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| macOS    | `.pkg`, with a chooser | VST3, Audio Unit, Standalone |
| Windows  | `.exe`                 | VST3, Standalone             |
| Linux    | none, use the zip      | VST3, LV2, Standalone        |

The macOS build is a universal binary and runs on both Apple Silicon and Intel. Logic and GarageBand only look for new Audio Units when they start, so restart them after installing.

On Linux the zip's contents go in `~/.vst3` and `~/.lv2`.

If you installed an earlier version with the package, the installer replaces it.

## What has actually been tried

All three platforms are built on every push, run through both test suites, and put through pluginval at strictness 8. The VST3 also goes through Steinberg's own conformance validator, and both suites run again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers. Adaptive was played in a real host on macOS, across many different mixes of partials, where the Audio Unit and the VST3 also pass `auval`. Nobody has run the Windows or Linux builds in a DAW yet, so treat those as untried and please open an issue with whatever you find.
