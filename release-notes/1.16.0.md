# Overtonium 1.16.0

A 32-partial additive synthesizer laid out like a 32-channel mixer. Every channel is one sine oscillator locked to a harmonic of the played note, and every channel has its own tuning, envelope and modulation.

## What is new in 1.16.0

**Two more characters measured from real instruments.** CHARACTER now has twelve choices.

**VCO** is the sine of an analogue voltage-controlled oscillator, recorded straight from its output, and the mixer it feeds. The sine is made from a triangle, so it has a second and a third harmonic near -25 dB, the same at every pitch. Each partial's pitch wanders by about a fifth of a cent, as the recorded oscillator's did, so a held chord is never quite still. The mixer was recorded driven from quiet to well past clipping. It stays clean until it reaches a rail, and its negative side gets there about two and a half times sooner than its positive side, so a chord played hard runs into it and gains an octave as well as a twelfth.

**Combo** is the 8' footage lever of a transistor combo organ. The organ divides one oscillator down for every octave of a note, which gives a square wave, and the lever is that square through a filter, so it has only odd harmonics. The filter stays put while the notes move, so the low notes keep more of the square: the third harmonic is -16 dB on the lowest C and -35 dB above C7. The organ filters each key once for all its levers, so every partial of a note takes the tone of the key you play, and the higher partials sound like the organ's higher levers. Its notes mix cleanly, and it carries the organ's faint hiss, which does not build up as you add keys.

**Three more output machines.** CLIP now has eight.

**Console** is a mixing console's mix bus, measured from a real chain at the settings it is used at: a transformer, a bus compressor and a tape stage, into the Limiter with its ceiling at -0.1 dBFS. The transformer thickens the lows as you drive it. The compressor is 4:1 and linked, with its sidechain filtered at 120 Hz so the bass does not pump it, and an automatic release that lets go fast after a short hit and slowly after a long one. The tape stage adds a steady third harmonic once it is driven.

**Cassette** is a four-track cassette recorder at 9.5 cm/s with Dolby B on a normal tape, measured by recording a test signal onto it and playing it back, into the Limiter with its ceiling at -0.1 dBFS. The lows saturate first and the top rolls off gently. Dolby lowers the tape's hiss by about 10 dB, and on this machine it does not quite cancel itself, so quiet highs come back a few decibels duller while loud ones pass almost untouched. The speed wanders by about a tenth of a percent, with flutter at 68 Hz and a slow wow. The hiss is there while something plays and gone when nothing does.

**Print** is Console recorded to Cassette, in the order the outboard chain and the deck were patched: the transformer, the compressor and the tape stage, a limiter at -0.1 dBFS, then the cassette recorder. From moderate levels the console's tape stage adds its steady third harmonic, where the cassette alone is still clean. At the top the compressor turns the loudest parts down before they reach the deck. The tape hiss sits 6 dB lower than on Cassette, as it does when the outboard chain puts the music onto the tape at a better level.

All three are held at the same level as the other machines for a quiet signal, so choosing one does not make a patch louder.

**Pick channels by their numbers (#75).** Cmd-click a channel's number, or Ctrl-click on Windows and Linux, to pick it, and drag across the numbers to pick a run. LINK's new **Selected** scope then moves only the channels you picked, so partials 3, 5, 9 and 12 can be moved together and nothing else. The selection is kept with your session, and Clear selection in the LINK menu empties it.

**A macro can move just the channels you pick.** Set a macro's scope to Selected channels and it takes the channels picked right now. The macro keeps its own copy, so picking other channels afterwards does not move it, and it is saved with the session and with your own presets. Undo puts a macro's channels back along with its scope.

**Three new presets.** _8-bit Choir_ is a breathy choir on 6581 at 16 kHz and 8 bits, with slow swells and glide between the notes. _Combo Organ_ is on Combo, with one vibrato for the whole keyboard and the modulation wheel or aftertouch bringing in the upper partials, into a bucket brigade echo and a spring reverb. _Tape Pluck_ is a plucked string on VCO over a quiet pad, printed to the cassette. Four presets moved to the new machines: _EP Chimes_ to Cassette, _KarplusStrong_ to Print, and _Space Cowboy_ and _Wurli_ to Console. Forty-one presets in all.

**Colours you can tell apart.** With twelve characters the old colours had run out of room. FM, PD and 6581 keep the colours of the instruments they were measured from, and the others are spread as far apart in hue as they go. The resonator, echo, reverb and output buttons now light in a colour for each machine instead of all in the same one, and the knobs beside each button light in its colour, so the bar shows what is chosen without reading it.

**Dark screens on a silenced channel.** On a muted channel, or one silenced by another's solo, the shape screens switch off instead of drawing the waveform in grey, like the readouts beside them.

**Easier to read MACROS.** The sentence at the top of the MACROS dialog is larger.

**Less CPU.** The engine uses about a tenth less: sixteen voices took 17.5% of one core and now take 15.5%. Patches with STRETCH save a little more. The output stage costs almost nothing unless Console, Cassette or Print is chosen, about 0.15% of a core at 48 kHz where it was 2.1 to 2.6%.

## Fixed

**The tool button showed the previous tool.** Choosing Pointer, Link or Draw from its menu left the old icon on the button until it was clicked again. It now changes at once.

**A preset file could silence the plugin.** A user preset holding a value that is not a number put it into the parameter, and every sample after that was not a number either. That value is now skipped and the rest of the file loads.

**The update notice opens only the project's own pages.** The link it opens comes from a file on the project's site, and anything that does not lead to the project's pages on GitHub is now replaced by the releases page.

## Under it

**Still 850 parameters.** VCO and Combo went on the end of CHARACTER's list, Console, Cassette and Print on the end of the clip type list, and Selected on the end of each macro's scope list, so every saved session and preset keeps what it had. The channels a macro on Selected reaches are not parameters: they are kept in the session and in a preset file beside the parameters.

**Measured, then checked against the recordings.** Each new character and machine was fitted to recordings of my own instruments and gear, and each was checked by running the same test signal through the code and through the same analysis as the recordings. The figures are in `Source/dsp/Measured.h`, `Source/dsp/Console.h` and `Source/dsp/Cassette.h`.

**Console, Cassette and Print run only while chosen.** Choosing one part way through a note fades to it over 30 ms with both running, so the switch does not click. None of them adds latency.

**A sweep of 2276 cases before release.** 1.15.0's cases with VCO and Combo among the setups, and each of the eight output machines driven hard at every one of eight sample rates from 8 kHz to 192 kHz and five block sizes from 1 to 4096 samples. Console, Cassette and Print also ran with the lookahead on, the output machine switched every 50 ms under ringing notes, and all forty-one presets went through six ways of playing. Every case renders finite, bounded audio and actually makes sound, and the whole sweep ran again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers.

**The build fetches what it uses by hash.** JUCE comes as the archive of one commit, checked against its SHA-256, and the validator the build runs is checked the same way.

## Upgrading from 1.15.0

**Automation of CHARACTER, the clip type and the macro scopes lands differently.** A host stores a choice as a fraction of the list's length. CHARACTER grows from ten choices to twelve, the clip type from five to eight and each macro's scope from fifteen to sixteen, so a lane drawn before 1.16.0 lands a little further along. Sessions and presets are unaffected, since every new choice went on the end.

**MIDI program changes from 5 up land later.** _8-bit Choir_ sorts at program 5, _Combo Organ_ at 8 and _Tape Pluck_ at 38, so 1.15.0's programs 5 and 6 move up one, 7 to 35 move up two, and 36 and 37 move up three. A saved session stores the preset's name as well as its number and is unaffected.

**Four presets sound different.** _EP Chimes_, _KarplusStrong_, _Space Cowboy_ and _Wurli_ are on their new output machines.

**The lamps have new colours.** Every character except FM, PD and 6581 lights in a different colour from before, and so does every machine on the resonator, echo, reverb and output buttons.

**Nothing else sounds different.** The faster engine rounds the oscillators' phase differently, by less than anyone can hear. Of the clips on the site other than those four presets, 13 moved by at most -83 dB under their peak, and the rest render bit for bit as they did in 1.15.0.

## Installing

The [install page](https://benjamindehli.github.io/overtonium/install/#downloads) has a row per file saying who each one is for. In short: take the installer for your platform, or the zip beside it if you have no administrator rights or keep plugins somewhere of your own.

| Platform | Installer              | Formats                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| macOS    | `.pkg`, with a chooser | VST3, Audio Unit, Standalone |
| Windows  | `.exe`                 | VST3, Standalone             |
| Linux    | none, use the zip      | VST3, LV2, Standalone        |
