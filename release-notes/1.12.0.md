# Overtonium 1.12.0

A 32-partial additive synthesizer laid out like a 32-channel mixer. Every channel is one sine oscillator locked to a harmonic of the played note, and every channel has its own tuning, envelope and modulation.

## What is new in 1.12.0

**Glide on every channel.** Issue #38 asked for a portamento with a knob for its time. Every channel has its own, the last row under PITCH MOD, from Off, where every channel starts, to five seconds. Because each partial travels on its own time, the series does not have to arrive together. Short times on the top and a long one on the fundamental land the brightness first and let the pitch follow. The other way round, the note arrives and its colour catches up. Somewhere between, the partials stop agreeing on a fundamental while they move and settle into one as they land, which no single portamento can do.

A partial glides in a straight line in pitch, into the same sum as the vibrato, the drift and both bends, so the oscillator character, the fade near Nyquist and the Bulb character's lamp all follow the pitch the partial has actually reached. The needle on each partial's PITCH MOD rule follows the glide too, swinging back to centre as the partial arrives, so a series given different glide times can be watched landing a partial at a time.

**Two switches over every channel**, as lit letters beside the GLIDE caption and again in the GLIDE knob's right-click menu. **L** is when: _Always_, which glides from the last note played even after its key has come up, or _Legato_, which glides only from a key still held, so a phrase played detached lands on each note and played joined slides through them. **T** is what the time means: _Fixed rate_, where the time is per octave and a wide leap takes longer than a step, the way a finger sliding along a string does, or _Fixed time_, where every slide takes the knob's time however far it goes. Both belong to the patch, so a legato lead can say how it glides, and both default to the first choice.

Where a glide starts is worked out before anything is stolen, since the voice a glide comes from can be the one about to be taken. Played polyphonically, a new note glides from the note played last. In Legato the voice carrying the phrase glides from wherever it is, so a key pressed halfway through a slide turns it round from where it had got to. The sustain pedal holds the sound and not the hand, so a note only the pedal is holding does not count as held.

**ATTACK reaches below its shortest time into where a partial starts.** The PHASE knob is gone, since on sine waves at thirty-two different tunings it did very little. What it was good for lives at the bottom of ATTACK instead. Turned past 0.2 ms the knob reads in degrees, from 0°, a rising zero crossing, down to 90°, which starts the partial on its own peak with no attack time at all, and the ring shows that stretch as dots rather than ticks. STRIKE moves along the whole knob, so a knob at 90° gives a hard blow the peak and lets a light one travel up through the crossing into a short attack. A host that lets you type a value takes `45°`, `5 ms` or `1.2 s`. The noise channel's attack is still a time, having no cycle to start in.

**Legato is a switch, and with more than one voice it plays a top line.** Issue #42 asked for a legato melody over chords that still retrigger, without having to lift every key. Legato sits in Settings beside the voice count. With one voice it is the monophonic legato the first entry in the voice list always was. With two to sixteen, a key landing above everything held except the highest note carries that note's phrase on: it starts a voice of its own that takes over the highest note's envelopes, modulators and drift where they stand and glides from its pitch, so it does not start again. Every other key plays as it always does, so a chord under the melody retriggers each time its keys come up and go down.

The note a melody carries on from is never taken away, so a key that turns out to belong to the chord always sounds. It goes on sounding while its key is down and fades over 20 ms when the key comes up. A key going down under the highest note could be the melody stepping down or a chord struck under a held melody, so the instrument goes by what is likely: above the chord when one is held, and within a major third and the highest of the keys struck together otherwise. A wrong guess costs an attack and nothing more, and no key ever waits. When the voices run out, the melody is the last note taken. With MPE on, a note carried onto a key of its own channel brings that key's bend, pressure and slide.

**Shared by all notes.** The switch that makes a modulator one circuit every note hears together, rather than one per note, was called "In phase across the keyboard", and it now says what it does. It has a switch of its own beside each SHAPE caption in the gutter, marked with three strokes merging into one, as well as its place in the shape menu.

**A thirty-fourth factory preset.** _Glide_ is a pad that arrives from the bottom up. Its glide times rise from nothing on the fundamental to two and a half seconds on the thirty-second, so every new note lands at once and its brightness slides in after it. Velocity reaches only the upper partials and STRIKE stretches a soft note's attack, so it stays round and slow played gently and turns bright and punchy played hard.

**The panel is quieter and easier to read.** The strips and the knob collars are a shade darker, and the dim text a shade lighter, so the captions in the gutter now clear the 4.5:1 contrast the accessibility standard asks for at their size, where they fell just short. The unlit caps are a warmer plastic with darker ink, the mute lamp is brighter so a muted channel still stands out clearly against them, and the screens carry a little more light behind their segments. The switches in the gutter are letters lit in a screen rather than raised caps, so they read as part of the column of captions instead of a second row of mute buttons. The tagline under the wordmark is a size larger.

## Fixed

**Every screen and lamp had a dark sliver at its top left corner.** The shadow inside a recess sits one pixel up and to the left of the opening, and it used a tighter corner than the lit edge around it, so at the top left it curved out past that edge onto bare panel, near black, which also made that corner look less round than the other three. On the round lamps it was a crescent. The shadow now stays inside the lit edge on every shape.

That is the whole of this section. Plenty else went wrong on the way here, but it was in work that had not shipped.

## Under it

**839 parameters.** The thirty-two start phase parameters became the thirty-two glide times, declared in the same places, so every other parameter keeps the position it has always had for a host that goes by position. The two glide switches and Legato went on the end.

**The Limiter no longer looks ahead unless asked.** Its two-millisecond window cost every instance 96 samples of latency whatever the clip setting, and only the Limiter uses it. New instances report 12 samples at 48 kHz, as they did before 1.11.0, and the lookahead is one tick away in Settings for anyone who uses the Limiter. No factory preset does.

**What the new engine costs.** Measured against 1.11.1 on the same machine, every factory preset with sixteen voices held, the mean goes from 13.6% of one core to 14.1%, which is the per-block work glide and the top line need. A computation the phase region added to every partial on every block, which would have cost three times that, was caught before release and is now done only when the knob moves, with every preset rendering bit for bit as it did.

**A sweep of 310 cases before release.** Pitch at eight sample rates from 8 kHz to 192 kHz and block sizes from 1 to 4096 samples, the top line with five-second glides at every one of them, nine settings against five ways of playing on four presets, parameters moved at random every block, and the extremes at both ends of the rate range. Every case renders finite, bounded audio and actually makes sound, and the sweep and both test suites ran clean under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers.

**The value a host shows for Glide Mode** reads _Fixed rate_ or _Fixed time_, as the menu does.

**The tagline and the description a host shows say "synthesizer"**, with a z, everywhere.

## Upgrading from 1.11.1

**New instances report 12 samples of latency at 48 kHz rather than 108**, since the Limiter's lookahead is now off by default. A session you saved keeps the setting it was saved with.

**Start phase is gone.** A session or patch that set one plays each partial from the zero crossing instead, which on most sounds is inaudible. _StyloPoly_ is the one factory preset where it shows: its partials used to start spread across the whole cycle, which partly cancelled at the onset, and its first 50 ms are about 1.8 dB peakier now. A quarter turn of ATTACK cannot cancel the way a whole cycle could, which was tried and measured worse. An automation lane drawn on a start phase in a host that goes by position now moves that channel's glide, and a macro aimed at the Phase row now drives Glide, which is the price of keeping every other lane and macro where it was.

**ATTACK automation drawn against the old travel lands on different times**, because the phase region now sits below the times on the knob. Saved sessions and presets store values rather than positions and are unaffected, and so is every factory preset.

**A MIDI program change may name a different preset.** Factory presets are numbered by their place in the alphabetical list and _Glide_ sorts after _Glass Armonica_, so every preset from _Glockenspiel_ on has moved up by one. A saved session carries the preset's name as well as its number, and the name is what decides.

**A session that chose Legato from the voice list** plays as Legato ticked with one voice, which is what that entry meant. The list no longer offers it.

**Your own patches get no glide**, Always and Fixed rate, which is what every patch written before this had, since the glide parameters are new and an old patch says nothing about them.

## Installing

The [install page](https://benjamindehli.github.io/overtonium/install/#downloads) has a row per file saying who each one is for. In short: take the installer for your platform, or the zip beside it if you have no administrator rights or keep plugins somewhere of your own.

| Platform | Installer              | Formats                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| macOS    | `.pkg`, with a chooser | VST3, Audio Unit, Standalone |
| Windows  | `.exe`                 | VST3, Standalone             |
| Linux    | none, use the zip      | VST3, LV2, Standalone        |

The macOS build is a universal binary and runs on both Apple Silicon and Intel. Logic and GarageBand only look for new Audio Units when they start, so restart them after installing.

On Linux the zip's contents go in `~/.vst3` and `~/.lv2`.

If you installed an earlier version with the package, the installer replaces it.

## What has actually been tried

All three platforms are built on every push, run through both test suites, and put through pluginval at strictness 8. The VST3 also goes through Steinberg's own conformance validator, and both suites run again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers. Glide was played on an Expressive E Osmose and the top line on an ordinary MIDI keyboard, both in a real host on macOS, where the Audio Unit and the VST3 also pass `auval`. Nobody has run the Windows or Linux builds in a DAW yet, so treat those as untried and please open an issue with whatever you find.
