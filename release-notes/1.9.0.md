# Overtonium 1.9.0

A 32-partial additive synthesiser laid out like a 32-channel mixer. Every channel is one sine oscillator locked to a harmonic of the played note, and every channel has its own tuning, envelope and modulation.

## What is new in 1.9.0

**Which oscillator the partials are.** A new control at the head of the series group on the bar, offering six of them. All six are sine oscillators. What differs is how each one fails to be one, which is the only thing that ever told two analogue oscillators apart: no circuit makes a mathematically perfect sine, and the ways each design misses are what people mean when they call one warm and another sterile.

_Pure_ is the sine every partial already was, and is what a patch gets unless it asks for something else.

_Bulb_ is a Wien bridge held steady by a lamp. It adds no harmonics at all, which is honest to the circuit, because holding the level steady is the lamp's whole job. What the lamp costs is time: it takes about half a second to settle, and the gain the loop needs changes with the frequency, so the level walks behind every move of the pitch. It answers gestures rather than sitting there. A vibrato is heard in the level as well as in the pitch, a bend dips and recovers as the lamp catches up, and a held note whose pitch is still sounds exactly like Pure.

_Squashed_ is a phase-shift oscillator leaning on its own rails, with a third harmonic at -23 dB and no even ones, since a limit that clips both halves alike cannot make them.

_Folded_ is a triangle bent into a sine by two diodes that do not match, so one half of the wave is a different shape from the other. That asymmetry puts a second, third and fourth harmonic on it, all near -32 dB, which is a kink rather than a warmth.

_Valve_ is a triode biased so one half leans over before the other. It is the only one here whose loudest addition is an octave above the partial rather than a twelfth, which is the whole of what people mean by valve warmth.

_Slewed_ is an amplifier that cannot move as fast as a fast note asks it to. It is the one that changes as you play: below a kilohertz nothing is being limited, and above it the partials lose first their corners and then everything but their slopes, which is a triangle. So the top of the series hardens as it climbs while the bottom stays where it was, and a chord played high is a different instrument from the same chord played low.

Because this is an additive instrument, those harmonics land among the partials you are already mixing, at exact multiples of the partial that made them. TUNE and STRETCH have moved the real partials off exact ratios, so the two beat against each other rather than adding up, which is what makes a character sound like a circuit rather than like a tone control.

It is part of the patch rather than part of the setup, so a preset can change it and saving one carries it. Every factory preset that ships is Pure.

**A MIDI program change loads a factory preset.** By its position in the alphabetical Factory list, counting from zero, so a keyboard, a foot controller or a clip can change the sound without anyone touching the menu. A number past the last preset is ignored rather than wrapping, and it arrives on any channel, MPE included. The preset loads whether or not it is the one already showing, which is what a program change does on an instrument with a panel, so a clip that opens with one sounds the same on every pass and anything you had changed by hand is replaced. Presets of your own have no number to be called by.

**The converter readouts say what they are counting.** They read `48.0 kHz` and `8 bit` where they used to have room for the figure alone. LINK moved to make that room: it now stands at the top of the caption column on the left, over the rows it gangs, and it is the one control that is not on the bar. With it gone from there the bar also fits on one row at the width the window opens at, where it used to take two, which gives the mixer 58 px of window back.

**Every switch on the panel lights its word rather than its face.** The two effect toggles, LINK, the character button and the M and S on all thirty-three channels. The face stays exactly as it is when the switch is off, and the word comes up lit with its light spilling onto the button around it. The character button lights in its own colour, from the yellow at the top of the mixer's own band for Bulb down to the red of channel 3 for Slewed, and not at all for Pure. The mute went from an orange to a red at the same time, which is also now the colour of the top of the output meter.

**The standalone wears the instrument's own window.** Its title bar is JUCE's rather than the platform's, so it used to be the grey-green every unstyled JUCE application wears, with a red cross and a yellow dash on it. It is now the same lit and grained panel as everything below it, with the name across the middle and the two marks drawn the way every other small mark here is: dim until the pointer is on them, and then lit.

## Fixed

**Undo took a step several times a second while you were still turning a knob.** A gesture was meant to be one step in the history however many values it moved and however long it went on, and it was, for a LINK drag across 32 channels. For one knob it was not: the history filled with the values the knob passed through on its way, so getting back to where you started took as many undos as the drag took seconds. It was most obvious with a scroll wheel, which is how most of this instrument gets adjusted.

The editor decides a gesture has ended by watching for stillness, and it was watching the wrong thing. The value tree folds repeated writes to one property into the action already there, so one knob under a scroll wheel holds the number of actions at one from the first write onwards, which reads as having stopped. It now times the stillness from the last parameter that actually moved. Measured on a note held across the line, a vibrato that used to tick at twice its own rate now steps no more than a plain sine does.

## Under it

**One parameter was added, 782 in all.** Character is appended after the existing ones rather than inserted among them, so every automation lane written before this release still points at the control it was pointing at.

**A character costs nothing per sample.** The oscillator is one interpolated table read, an envelope tick and a gain, running 512 times per sample at 16 voices, so a waveshaper in that loop would be the whole engine again and the oversampling needed to stop it aliasing would be three more. Instead each circuit is run over a sine once at startup, the harmonics it leaves are read off, and a table is built from them. Measured against Pure at eight voices, four of the others come out within a couple of percent, which is inside the spread between two runs of the same binary, and Slewed at about five, because one note's partials fall in different bands and read several tables where the others read one.

The harmonics alias like any others, so each character is built several times over, each stopping at a different harmonic, and a partial reads the highest one that still fits under Nyquist at the pitch it is at that moment. A partial near the top of the range plays a plain sine whatever is chosen. The tables come to about 400 kB and take about 8 ms to build, once, when the plugin is prepared.

## Upgrading from 1.8.0

Patches, presets and sessions load and sound exactly as they did. Character defaults to Pure, which is the oscillator every partial already was, and a preset or session that says nothing about it gets that.

**Automation is unaffected.** The one new parameter went on the end, and nothing else moved.

**The standalone remembers its window size**, as it always has, because the editor's size travels in the plugin's state. If you left it small it opens small. Drag it out once and that sticks, or on macOS delete `~/Library/Application Support/Overtonium.settings` to start again.

## Installing

The [install page](https://benjamindehli.github.io/overtonium/install/#downloads) has a row per file saying who each one is for. In short: take the installer for your platform, or the zip beside it if you have no administrator rights or keep plugins somewhere of your own.

| Platform | Installer              | Formats                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| macOS    | `.pkg`, with a chooser | VST3, Audio Unit, Standalone |
| Windows  | `.exe`                 | VST3, Standalone             |
| Linux    | none, use the zip      | VST3, LV2, Standalone        |

The macOS build is a universal binary and runs on both Apple Silicon and Intel. Logic and GarageBand only look for new Audio Units when they start, so restart them after installing.

On Linux the zip's contents go in `~/.vst3` and `~/.lv2`.

If you installed an earlier version with the package, the installer replaces it. A plugin folder holding two copies of Overtonium is worth tidying: macOS registers one of them and the host uses whichever that is, which need not be the newer.

## What has actually been tried

All three platforms are built on every push, run through both test suites, and put through pluginval at strictness 8. The VST3 also goes through Steinberg's own conformance validator, and both suites run again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers. macOS is the only platform where the plugin has been loaded into a real host, where the Audio Unit and the VST3 also pass `auval`. Nobody has run the Windows or Linux builds in a DAW yet, so treat those as untried and please open an issue with whatever you find.

Two things here came back from playing the instrument rather than from a test. The undo fix was reported as a scroll wheel filling the history, and the lamp in Bulb was inaudible on its first pass: a model faithful to a bench oscillator does nothing at the scale this instrument moves pitch at, which is cents rather than the decade a frequency dial is swept across. Both have tests now, written after the fact, which is the honest order.

## Windows will warn you

The Windows installer is not signed, so SmartScreen says the publisher is unknown. Choose "More info" and then "Run anyway". If you would rather not, build from source: the readme says how, and it takes one CMake command.

## macOS

The installer is signed and notarised, so it opens without a warning. It asks which of the three formats you want and installs only those.
