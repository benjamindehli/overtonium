# Overtonium 1.10.0

A 32-partial additive synthesiser laid out like a 32-channel mixer. Every channel is one sine oscillator locked to a harmonic of the played note, and every channel has its own tuning, envelope and modulation.

## What is new in 1.10.0

**The echo is three machines, and so is the reverb.** Both were a switch that turned one thing on. Both are now a button that says which machine is running, with an Off at the top of the list, and the panel underneath each is unchanged.

_Tape_ is the loop that was already there. _BBD_ is a line of buckets handing a charge along, and the thing that makes those worth keeping is that they get darker the longer you set them to: the line holds a fixed number of buckets, so a longer delay can only be had by clocking them more slowly, and the filter that reconstructs the signal has to come down with the clock. Short settings are nearly clean and long ones are murk, which is a second job TIME has on this type and on no other. _Digital_ is the one that does not pretend to be anything, and it is the only ping-pong of the three: the input arrives in the middle and every repeat crosses to the other side, where the tape and the bucket brigade never cross at all.

AGE is what makes three machines worth having rather than three names for one. It is the same knob on all of them and it asks each for the thing that machine gets worse at. On the tape that is wear: top end lost on every pass, motor wander, and how hard the tape leans over. On the bucket brigade it is the clock slowing and the compander breathing, so hiss swells in behind a chord and ducks away as the repeats die. On the digital it is bits and sample rate, down to six bits and an eighth of the host rate, so a tail starts as the signal and ends as a memory of it. At nothing the digital is exactly a copy, which is what a digital delay is for.

_Room_ is the reverb that was already there. _Modulated plate_ is a sheet of steel with no geometry to hear, so a hit spreads across all of it at once and the tail is dense from its first instant rather than arriving in stages: measured on a click, the first fiftieth of a second carries one and a half times the average of the whole tail where the room carries none of it. It is called modulated because it is. Its allpasses wander six times as far as the shape they come from gives them, and without that a long decay repeats its own pattern instead of washing, which is audible as a delay with some reverb on it. _Spring_ is a tray of three helical springs, and what makes a spring a spring is that a helix carries the top of the band faster than the bottom, so a hit comes back not as a hit but as a chirp that starts high and falls. Every bounce adds another, so a spring gets less like what you played as it decays rather than merely quieter.

DECAY, DAMP and PRE mean the same thing on all three reverbs, and that is checked rather than hoped for: at the same setting the three tails run 2.23, 2.38 and 2.46 seconds. The wet levels are matched to a hundredth of a decibel as well, so switching machines changes the character of a tail and not its length or its loudness.

**The character now says how hard the mix is driven.** The thirty-three channels were simply added together. They are now summed onto a bus that drives, by an amount belonging to the oscillator character, because the character is meant to say what circuit you are playing through and a summing amplifier is part of that circuit.

It answers to level rather than to a setting, which is what a table baked into one partial's waveform cannot do: play quietly and it is barely there, lean on the keyboard and it arrives. Bulb asks for the most and Diode the least, Pure sums cleanly and does nothing at all, and there is no knob for it, because how hard a summing amplifier is driven is part of what choosing a character means. Every factory preset on a character therefore has a little more in it than it had, and that is the intended sound of them.

**How fast you let a key go sets the level its tail starts from.** On a keyboard that senses a release, the hardest lift doubles the key-off level and the softest halves it, geometrically, so two steps down and two steps up undo each other. There is no knob and no parameter: it is a property of the gesture the way velocity is, and nothing about a patch changes because of it. A keyboard that cannot sense a release changes nothing, including the two different things "no release velocity" looks like on the wire, a note-off carrying zero and a note-on of velocity zero, both of which are read as no information rather than as the softest possible lift.

**The master volume is a glass fader lying across the output meter.** It was a knob in a group of its own. It now lies over the lamps it is setting the level of, standing a little proud of them top and bottom the way the channel faders stand proud of their tracks, and the group it used to need is gone. The two converter readouts moved up with it and sit a little off the bottom of the panel rather than flush against it.

**A thirty-second factory preset, and four others that reach for the new machines.** _60s Organ_ is a combo organ that has not been serviced: the partials are pulled off each other rather than tuned, so the stack beats against itself instead of fusing, and the noise channel is a few milliseconds of key click on every note. It runs into a bucket brigade at nearly eight tenths of feedback, driven far enough in that the repeats grow rather than fade, and falls into a spring long enough to still be ringing when the next chord arrives. It is also the first patch in the set to ask for Diode, which had come out of the character pass with nothing at all.

_Shimmer_ and _Tape Choir_ now ask for the plate rather than the room, which is what a long wet tail over a moving spectrum wants. _Cathedral_ and _Struck Bell_ stay on the room. The other twenty-seven use no reverb or echo at all and are unaffected.

**LINK came back switched off.** The scope and the curve were remembered with the session and the switch was not, so reopening a window gave you exactly the drag you had set up, turned off. All three are saved together now, and switching it off sticks as well, which is the half a fix that only wrote the setting when it was true would have missed.

**Unfolding a section made the faders swell.** On a window shrunk down until the faders were squeezed, folding a section behaved and unfolding it gave back twice the height it had taken, all of it to the faders, which on a Mac put them under the dock. Applying the window's new size limits is itself a resize, so the height was being measured after it had already moved and the same rows were counted twice. Folding never showed it, because folding lowers the limit rather than raising it and nothing gets constrained.

**A section folds from any strip, not just from the gutter.** Every section draws a rule across all thirty-three columns, lined up with its heading in the caption gutter on the far left. Clicking one of those rules now folds that section, the same as clicking the heading. Reaching the gutter meant leaving the partial you were working on, crossing the mixer and finding your way back, and by then you had lost which column you were in. Nothing needed making clickable for it: the rules are the one part of a strip with nothing standing on them, and the lamps four of the five carry already let clicks through so the rule underneath reads as a line rather than a gap. The pointer shows the same hand over a rule as over a heading.

**The bar says what each group is.** A small caption under the button at the head of the character, echo and reverb groups. Those buttons show a value, so the bar reads OP-AMP, TAPE and ROOM with nothing saying what any of them is a choice of. That was tolerable while each was one fixed thing behind a switch, and it is not with three machines on each of two of them.

With the caption doing that job, the button is free to say the one thing the caption cannot. It reads OFF when the effect is off, where it used to read ECHO or REVERB so that the group could still be found. The ring of lit ticks around that effect's four knobs goes dark at the same time: they keep their positions, so a setting can still be dialled in before the thing is switched on, but nothing on a stage that is not in the signal is lit.

**The clipper has a switch on the bar.** CLIP, under the output meter beside the two converter readouts, which are now a little larger and sit hard up under the meter. Those three are the whole of what happens to the signal on its way out, so they stand together. It was only in the Settings menu before, on the grounds that it is set once and left, which is true of where you end up and not of how you get there: whether the output is being caught is a question about what you are hearing right now. It is still in the menu as well, and both follow the one parameter.

**A LINK drag that spreads a move uniformly now spreads it in decibels.** On the fader row it was uniform in travel, which is not uniform in anything you can hear: dragging the loudest channel up put six or seven decibels on it and thirty on the quietest. It is the same number of decibels on every channel now, so the drag is a level change and not a change in the sound.

## Fixed

**The first repeat of an echo came back clean.** Every repeat after it was worn and the first was not, on both the tape and the bucket brigade, so the machine seemed to switch on only once you had heard it work. The output was being taken from the delay line ahead of the stage that ages it. Both now take the coloured signal.

**Both echoes compressed everything they were handed.** The tape's backstop, which exists so that a steady tone at ninety-five percent feedback cannot pile up, was set for a bus that ran near one. The bus runs at one and a half to nearly two on a full patch, so the backstop was permanently engaged and the repeats were squashed at every setting rather than only at the extreme it was there to catch. It is set for the level the bus actually reaches now, and both echoes are cleaner for it.

**The digital delay had a tail that never went away**, at any feedback from a half upwards, still audible twenty-five seconds later. A quantiser inside a feedback loop has a fixed point: one step times a feedback of a half rounds back up to one step. The wear is applied on the way out now rather than on the way round, which loses nothing, because the step stays the same size while the repeats get quieter, so a late repeat is still crushed against a coarser grid than an early one.

**The plugin would not tell you which version it was.** Not in the Settings menu, not on the panel, not anywhere, while the bug report template refused a report without a version and told people to read it off the Settings menu or the credit line under the wordmark. It was in neither and never had been. The menu now opens with the version as its first heading, and the templates say where to find it.

**A scroll over a knob also dragged the mixer sideways**, whenever the window was narrow enough for the series to need scrolling. Each strip watches everything inside it so that a pointer resting on a knob is reported by the strip rather than swallowed by the control, and a watcher like that is handed every event, wheels included, which it then passed on upward. So the knob took the scroll and the panel took it again. The wheel is how most of this instrument gets adjusted, so this was worth more than its size. A scroll on a strip's background still moves the series, which is the half a careless fix would have broken.

**Right-clicking the gap between two sections opened two LINK menus**, stacked on each other. Picking an item on the front one left the one behind it standing with its ticks unmoved, so the setting looked to have been refused while the instrument had already taken it. A strip watches everything inside it so that a right-click on a knob reaches the LINK menu rather than being swallowed by the control, and a watcher like that is handed the strip's own clicks as well as its children's, so a click on the strip itself arrived twice. Only the gaps between sections showed it, since everywhere else on a strip has a control over it.

**The zoom menu ticked 100% whatever the zoom was.** The zooming itself was right and the tick was not, so the menu disagreed with the window.

**_Sparkle Pad_ was mixed low on purpose and no longer needs to be.** Its channels were deliberately quiet to keep the tape echo's distortion down, which is the thing that was wrong above. They are back up, by the same number of decibels on every channel.

## Under it

**Two parameters were added, 786 in all.** Echo Type and Reverb Type, both on the end, so every automation lane written before this release still points at the control it was pointing at. The switch that turns each effect on is the older parameter and stayed where it was, which is why a patch made before this release loads with the tape it was made on and the room it was made in.

**The bus drive runs at twice the sample rate**, through a half-band filter, so nothing it makes folds back down. It is antiderivative antialiased on top of that, and two things about it had to be measured rather than reasoned about: moving the amount spiked the output, because the antiderivative was being carried across blocks instead of recomputed, and at small drives the gain was more than a decibel out, which was float cancellation rather than anything about the curve.

**The op-amp's drive on the bus is a corner rather than a rate limit.** A slew limit is the right model for an amplifier that cannot keep up and the wrong one for a bus, where it reacts to the sum's slope rather than to any one note: it broke up on high notes at settings that sounded fine on low ones, measuring around -60 dB of inharmonic rubbish across the keyboard. A cubic corner does what was wanted without the register deciding how much.

**The spring is the one expensive thing here.** Its dispersion is a chain of allpasses per spring and there are three springs, so it costs about as much as two voices where the room and the plate each cost a fraction of one. The chain started at a hundred sections and five percent of a core; sixty sections with steeper coefficients keep nine tenths of the chirp for under half the work, and the table of what was tried is in the source beside the number.

## Upgrading from 1.9.0

**Presets on a character sound fuller**, because the bus they are summed onto now drives. That is the intended sound of them and there is no switch to take it off, short of choosing Pure. A session is unaffected in the sense that every value it stored comes back, but the bus is part of the instrument rather than part of the patch, so a session on a character will have it too.

**The echoes are cleaner and their first repeat is coloured.** If you had a patch leaning on the tape echo's compression, it is not there any more.

**_Sparkle Pad_ is louder** and otherwise unchanged. Loading it gives what it ships as rather than what it was in 1.9.0; a session that had it loaded carries the values and is not affected.

**A MIDI program change may name a different preset.** Factory presets are numbered by their position in the alphabetical list, and _60s Organ_ sorts second, so every preset after it has moved up by one. A clip or a foot controller sending a number will pick the sound above the one it used to. Sessions are not affected: a saved session carries the preset's name as well as its index, and the name is what decides.

**Automation is unaffected.** The two new parameters went on the end, and nothing else moved.

**Your own patches get the tape and the room**, which is what every patch written before this had, because the type is a new parameter and an old patch says nothing about it.

## Installing

The [install page](https://benjamindehli.github.io/overtonium/install/#downloads) has a row per file saying who each one is for. In short: take the installer for your platform, or the zip beside it if you have no administrator rights or keep plugins somewhere of your own.

| Platform | Installer              | Formats                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| macOS    | `.pkg`, with a chooser | VST3, Audio Unit, Standalone |
| Windows  | `.exe`                 | VST3, Standalone             |
| Linux    | none, use the zip      | VST3, LV2, Standalone        |

The macOS build is a universal binary and runs on both Apple Silicon and Intel. Logic and GarageBand only look for new Audio Units when they start, so restart them after installing.

On Linux the zip's contents go in `~/.vst3` and `~/.lv2`.

If you installed an earlier version with the package, the installer replaces it. A plugin folder holding two copies of Overtonium is worth tidying: macOS registers one of them and the host uses whichever that is, which need not be the newer.

## What has actually been tried

All three platforms are built on every push, run through both test suites, and put through pluginval at strictness 8. The VST3 also goes through Steinberg's own conformance validator, and both suites run again under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers. macOS is the only platform where the plugin has been loaded into a real host, where the Audio Unit and the VST3 also pass `auval`. Nobody has run the Windows or Linux builds in a DAW yet, so treat those as untried and please open an issue with whatever you find.

How much drive each character puts on the bus was decided by ear, at a keyboard, one character at a time, with a development control that is not in this build. There is no measurement behind those five numbers and there should not be. The same goes for which of the three echoes and which of the three reverbs any preset would want, which is why no preset was changed to ask for one: the new machines are there to be reached for rather than shipped switched on.

Most of what is fixed above came back from playing rather than from a test, and the tests were written afterwards, which is the honest order. The first repeat coming back clean, the echoes squashing everything, the digital delay's tail that never went away, and the plate sounding like a delay at a long decay were all heard first. The plate is the one worth naming twice: it passed every test it had, and the test that would have caught it had to be invented to describe what was wrong with it.

## Windows will warn you

The Windows installer is not signed, so SmartScreen says the publisher is unknown. Choose "More info" and then "Run anyway". If you would rather not, build from source: the readme says how, and it takes one CMake command.

## macOS

The installer is signed and notarised, so it opens without a warning. It asks which of the three formats you want and installs only those.
